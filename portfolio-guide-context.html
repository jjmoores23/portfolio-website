Betrayal and Resistance Theory Found in Othello

A 2024 essay arguing that Iago and Othello become products of resistance theory. It studies three defining developments: Iago's setup of a drunken Cassio, Iago's manipulation of Othello, and the fatal final scene. The conclusion contrasts Iago's destructive resistance and downfall with Cassio's eventual promotion to lord governor.

Works cited include William Shakespeare's Othello and scholarship by Alaeddin Nahvi, David Houston Wood, Veronika Walker, and Jennifer Feather.

Reading page | Complete plain text

Conformity is an Expression of Insecurity

A 2022 essay arguing that conformity exposes insecurity in three groups: leaders fear disorder and enforce rules, working-class conformists fear retaliation and remain obedient, and anarchists fear losing individuality by blending into the crowd. The Chinese Social Credit System is used as the main real-world example of enforced conformity.

Works cited: Drew Donnelly's explanation of China's Social Credit System; Barbara Gray and Jennifer J. Kish-Gephart on social class at work; and Theodore John Kaczynski's Industrial Society and Its Future.

Reading page | Complete plain text

Juxtaposing Characters Skills for Navigating British Imperialism and Slavery

A 2025 comparison of Tambu in Tsitsi Dangarembga's Nervous Conditions, William Thornhill in Kate Grenville's The Secret River, and Adaora in Nnedi Okorafor's Lagoon. Tambu tries to benefit from education while recognizing imperial harm; Thornhill becomes an executor of colonial violence against the Darug people; Adaora favors evidence and peaceful integration when aliens arrive in Lagos. The essay argues that their different approaches show no single response to imperialism and slavery.

Works cited: Nervous Conditions, The Secret River, and Lagoon.

The Elimination of General Studies at MUN

A 2022 position essay arguing that Memorial University should remove the general-studies year from first-year programs. Its main arguments concern the cost of courses that do not contribute to program entry and the study time they take away from required courses. It acknowledges the counterargument that general courses can ease the transition from high school to university.

Sources include Memorial University admissions and tuition material, David Sorensen, Donna McGee-Thompson, Nick Huntington-Klein and Andrew Gill, and the Government of Newfoundland and Labrador.

The Gradual Regression of Violence Censorship in Romanticism

A 2025 essay tracing increasingly direct depictions of violence through Percy Bysshe Shelley's Ode to the West Wind, Christina Rossetti's Goblin Market, and Wilfred Owen's Dulce et Decorum Est. Shelley uses restrained morbid imagery, Rossetti makes assault central while retaining euphemism, and Owen depicts battlefield trauma without that restraint. The essay connects this progression to the continuation of Romantic passion and intensity beyond Wordsworth.

Works cited: poems by Owen, Rossetti, and Shelley.

Universal Indifference Regarding Uplifting the Impoverished

A 2025 comparison of Ursula K. Le Guin's The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas and Katherine Mansfield's The Garden Party. It argues that the privileged residents of Omelas and the Sheridan family understand the suffering near them but preserve their own happiness through conscious indifference. The imprisoned child in Omelas and the Scott family's poverty expose the human tendency to protect a comfortable social equilibrium rather than uplift others. Laura is the strongest exception, although her sympathy does not produce lasting material change.

Works cited: Le Guin's story and Mansfield's story.
